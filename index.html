Everything you glance at in the dark.

Clocks

Digital, analog, frame, flip, mono, float, text and world clock, with colors set per part and presets like "Night red." Held upright, the clocks stack hours over minutes; turned sideways they glide back onto one line. The flip clock gives every digit its own hinged card, and a digit only flips when it changes.

Weather & calendar

Animated weather scenes from your location, with four pages: now, Fahrenheit, forecast and a second city. The calendar shows today, the month or upcoming meetings, read-only from the Mac or iPhone Calendar app, or from a pasted Google, Outlook, Zoho or iCloud link on Android and Windows.

Summary

Up to eight cards on one panel. Hold a card and they jiggle like the iOS home screen: remove, add, drag to move, and pull a corner to resize.

Music & Spotify

What's playing, with transport controls, seeking, search on the app's own keyboard, the queue, playlists, device switching and volume. Timed lyrics scroll with the song, and Indian-language lyrics can be shown in English letters. Swipe the cover to skip; it turns over when the song changes.

Home

Smart lights, plugs and an HDMI sync box from four brands on one card, laid out like Apple Home: routines along the top, devices below.

Timer

A digital or flip countdown with a small figure running along a track. When time is up, the player scores and the confetti flies.

Desktop cards (Mac)

Live Clock, Timer, Music and Home cards that sit on the Mac desktop behind your windows, each with its own settings.

Night & battery

Brightness that follows the room light on Android, a night mode that fades to dim red with no blue light, an OLED burn-in shift, and battery rings for the device and its Bluetooth accessories.

Built like it matters.

The problems that took the longest, and what I did about them.

Spotify said yes and played nothing

Spotify's "play this" returns 204 (accepted) even when its player then loads nothing. Hushdock checks the player state after every play request and tells the user what Spotify reported. Each platform needed its own path: the Mac desktop app ignores remote play while stopped, so Hushdock queues the song and skips to it; on iPhone it tries Spotify's iOS SDK first.

Shared rate limits

Every install on one Spotify account shares one quota. The app slows its polling when only a desktop card is watching or nothing is playing, and when Spotify says "too many requests" it waits exactly as long as Spotify asks.

A race found by a model checker

Unplug the phone during the 700 ms launch delay and the app still opened, and nothing ever closed it. TLC found it by trying every order of events. Fixed in Kotlin, the decision logic pulled into a plain class, and TLC's failing traces replayed as JUnit tests. In total: 4 safety bugs and 1 liveness gap, all fixed.

Battery and smoothness

The clock redraws only when a digit changes, nothing animates while the window is hidden, and card changes slide a pre-drawn picture so even blurred, glass-heavy cards move smoothly on a large monitor.
